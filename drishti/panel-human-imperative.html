The Human Imperative

Purpose, Creativity and Agency in the Age of AI
Day 1 · Fri 13 Nov 202616:20 – 17:20IIM Bangalore, BengaluruTentative schedule
Register on Unstop ↗Full agenda
The central question

What must India do in the next decade to ensure AI expands human purpose, creativity and agency—rather than diminishing them—on the path to 2047?

Context & overview

Why this conversation, why now.

Artificial intelligence is moving beyond assistance into creation, analysis and increasingly autonomous action. As machines write, design, code, advise and act, the challenge is no longer only what technology can do, but what it means for people to exercise judgment, find meaning and make an original contribution.

For India, the next decade will shape how AI enters a vast and diverse society. Used well, AI could democratise expertise, unlock entrepreneurship and amplify creativity. Used poorly, it risks deskilling workers, deepening divides and concentrating choices in systems that people neither understand nor control.

‘The Human Imperative’ asks a bigger question than whether AI will replace jobs: what kind of work, leadership, culture and society should we deliberately build alongside capable machines? Productivity matters, but so do dignity, curiosity, human connection and the freedom to shape one’s own future.

Key discussion themes

Five questions on the table.

01Choose

Human Agency, Judgment & Accountability

As AI moves from advising to acting, which decisions must remain human—and how do we preserve autonomy, consent and responsibility?

02Belong

Purpose, Identity & the Dignity of Work

As roles and careers are redefined, how can individuals and institutions sustain meaning, motivation and a sense of contribution?

03Create

Imagination, Culture & Originality

In a world of machine-generated content, how do we nurture human taste, empathy, storytelling and breakthrough thinking rather than algorithmic sameness?

04Learn

Education, Skills & Cognitive Independence

How should schools, universities and employers cultivate critical thinking, self-direction and lifelong learning without fostering dependence on AI?

05Shape

Human-Centred Institutions & Decisive Moves

What must leaders and policymakers change by 2035 to safeguard choice, broaden access and ensure AI serves people rather than the reverse?

The panel

Who will be on the panel.

Four leaders offering complementary perspectives across organisational practice, ethical judgment, behavioural research and AI development. Panelists to be announced.

Corporate HR Head / CHRO

AI-led organisational change, meaningful work, employee motivation and people-centred leadership.

AI Ethicist / Philosopher

Human autonomy, consent, dignity and accountability in algorithmic decisions and autonomous systems.

OBHR Research Expert

Organisational behaviour research on motivation, identity, creativity, trust and human–AI collaboration.

AI Company Leader

AI capabilities, human augmentation and design choices that preserve independent judgment and user control.

Expected outcome

A human-centred agenda for India’s next decade—so that capable machines expand, rather than diminish, our purpose, creativity and agency.

The panel seeks to define the judgments that must remain ours, ways to protect purpose and creativity, learning that builds cognitive independence, and the organisational, technological and policy decisions required by 2035 to keep people in control on the path to 2047.
