The fundamental question

What decisive moves must India make today to establish itself as a global deep-tech leader by 2047?

Context & overview

Why this conversation, why now.

As India advances towards its centenary of independence in 2047, its aspirations to emerge as a global economic and technological powerhouse will depend increasingly on its ability to develop, commercialise and scale breakthrough technologies. Deep-tech innovations are poised to redefine industries, reshape global value chains, and determine the competitiveness of nations.

India stands at a pivotal juncture. With a growing startup ecosystem, expanding digital infrastructure, a strong engineering talent pool, and increasing policy emphasis on indigenous innovation, the country has an opportunity to transition from being predominantly a consumer and adopter of global technologies to becoming a creator of foundational technologies and intellectual property.

Yet realising this ambition demands overcoming significant structural challenges. Deep-tech ventures face long development cycles, high capital requirements, uncertain commercialisation pathways, and the complexities of translating scientific research into scalable businesses. Building globally competitive capabilities will require coordinated efforts across industry, academia, government, investors and entrepreneurs.

Key discussion themes

Five questions on the table.

01

India’s Deep-Tech Opportunity

Where can India build globally differentiated capabilities and technological leadership? Which emerging technologies offer the greatest strategic and economic potential?

02

From Research to Commercialisation

How can India bridge the gap between scientific research, intellectual property creation, product development, and commercially viable businesses?

03

Capital, Talent & Ecosystem Development

How can patient capital, specialised talent, research infrastructure, and academia–industry collaboration accelerate deep-tech innovation?

04

Technological Sovereignty & Global Competitiveness

How should India balance indigenous technological capabilities with integration into global innovation ecosystems and supply chains?

05

The Decisive Moves for 2047

What critical investments, institutional reforms, industry partnerships, and policy interventions must India prioritise over the coming decade?

The panel

Who will be on the panel.

Four to five distinguished leaders representing complementary perspectives across India’s deep-tech ecosystem. The final composition will seek diversity across technological domains, institutional perspectives and industry experience. Panelists to be announced.

Deep-Tech Entrepreneur / Founder

Founder or CEO of a pioneering Indian deep-tech venture in areas such as space technology, semiconductors, AI, robotics or biotechnology.

Technology & Industry Leader

CTO, chief scientist or R&D leader from a major technology, engineering or advanced manufacturing organisation driving indigenous innovation.

Research & Academic Leader

Distinguished scientist, research institution leader or academic entrepreneur with experience translating frontier research into real-world applications.

Deep-Tech Investor

Senior venture capital, private equity or strategic investment leader specialising in deep-tech ventures, patient capital and commercialisation.

Policy & Ecosystem Leader

Senior policymaker, technology mission leader or national innovation ecosystem leader involved in strengthening India’s technological capabilities.

Expected outcome

Moving beyond technological possibilities and aspirational narratives to identify actionable priorities for building India’s deep-tech future.

The conversation will examine what India must build, invest in, collaborate on, and fundamentally rethink to transform its technological potential into sustained global leadership, as an engine of economic growth, strategic resilience and industrial competitiveness on the road to Viksit Bharat 2047.
