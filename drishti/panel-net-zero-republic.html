Net-Zero Republic

India’s Blueprint for Sustainable Prosperity

The central question

What must India change in the next decade to build a prosperous, climate-resilient economy by 2047 while staying on course for net zero by 2070?

Context & overview

Why this conversation, why now.

India’s ambition to become a developed nation by 2047 intersects with its commitment to achieve net-zero emissions by 2070. The challenge goes far beyond cleaner power: the choices India makes on industrialisation, infrastructure, mobility, agriculture, land, water and resource use will shape whether growth is affordable, competitive, inclusive and resilient to a changing climate.

NITI Aayog’s 2026 integrated scenarios connect the country’s development aspirations with transformation across the sectors below. India must expand access to jobs and modern services while avoiding carbon-intensive lock-in and preparing for heat, floods and water stress. This is not simply an environmental agenda; it is a question of national economic design.

‘Net-Zero Republic’ therefore explores a new development compact: how governments, businesses, investors and citizens can make sustainability a source of productivity, energy and resource security, better living standards, and shared prosperity.

Key discussion themes

Five questions on the table.

01Grow

Growth, Competitiveness & Energy Security

How can India industrialise and create jobs while reducing emissions intensity, ensuring affordable energy and strengthening its position in green global value chains?

02Rebuild

Economic Systems for a New Era

What must change across power, manufacturing, mobility, logistics, cities and buildings—and how can efficiency, circularity and new technologies prevent long-term carbon and resource lock-in?

03Protect

Resources, Nature & Climate Resilience

How can India safeguard food and water security, farmer incomes, ecosystems, public health and climate-vulnerable communities amid rising heat and extreme weather?

04Finance

Capital, Markets & Institutions

What mix of green finance, investment incentives, credible carbon markets, public policy and Centre–State–city coordination will make transition viable at scale?

05Include

A Just Transition & the Decisive Moves

How do we protect affordability, MSMEs, workers and coal-dependent regions—and which investments and reforms must India prioritise between now and 2035?

The panel

Who will be on the panel.

An economy-wide conversation with four to five leaders offering complementary perspectives. Panelists to be announced.

Economic & Policy Leader

National development strategy, climate policy, public institutions or state-level execution.

Industry & Infrastructure Leader

Large-scale industrial transformation, manufacturing, mobility, energy or urban infrastructure.

Climate Resilience Leader

Agriculture, water, natural resources, cities or climate adaptation.

Sustainable Finance Leader

Long-horizon capital, banking, transition finance, markets or investment risk.

Just Transition / Development Leader

Livelihoods, MSMEs, workforce transition, social protection or inclusive growth.

Expected outcome

An integrated agenda for the next decade—so that prosperity by 2047 strengthens, rather than competes with, India’s net-zero pathway.

Beyond sector-specific climate pledges, the panel seeks to identify an integrated set of business choices, investments, policy reforms and social safeguards for the next decade.
